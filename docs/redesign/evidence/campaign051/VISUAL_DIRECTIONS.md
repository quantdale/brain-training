# Campaign 051 — Visual Directions

Three distinct directions were explored before implementation. Scores are
1–5; the score is a decision aid, not a claim of user research.

## Direction A — Signal Arcade (selected)

Ink navy and warm paper frame signal coral, electric cyan, voltage yellow, and
mint. Home is a pocket console; Games is a poster storefront; each domain has
a world and each mechanic has a geometric signature. Surfaces use blocks,
clipped/stepped edges, small radii, and one physical button lip. Dark mode is
an ink performance canvas with paper panels rather than an all-black neon wall.

## Direction B — Signal Lab

Near-black graphite, off-white data blocks, cyan/lime/coral instrument traces,
target rings, waveforms, and precise modular panels. Gameplay becomes a quiet
training laboratory. This is the most adult/credible direction, but it risks
clinical/cyberpunk associations and is less inviting for a first-time teen.

## Direction C — Bright Studio

Airy white, cobalt, tomato, sunflower, and mint with editorial cut-paper
illustrations, progress paths, badges, and a friendly studio tone. It is
approachable and legible, but without strict geometry it drifts toward generic
learning/wellness patterns and loses the training-console premise.

## Choice

**Lock Direction A — Signal Arcade.** It is the only direction that makes the
catalog itself the product object while still leaving a credible, quiet path
for Progress and Data Management. Direction B's restraint and Direction C's
friendliness are bounded references inside the lock, not averaged visual
directions.
